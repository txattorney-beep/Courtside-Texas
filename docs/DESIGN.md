# Courtside Texas: editorial identity

Introduced November 15, 2026, at Eric Hudson's request for a more distinctive, less generic design. The identity is a Texas litigation field guide: masthead, numbered contents, open court listings, editorial side notes, and ruled divisions. Preserve practical search and mobile access.

## Design source

- `public/brand-mark.svg`: editable gavel and open-C monogram, Courtside wordmark, tracked Texas line, and parallel signature rules.
- `public/favicon.svg`: simplified monogram for small sizes.
- `public/styles.css`: paper (#f5f0e6), ink (#222820), brick (#99462e), warm rules (#ccc5b6); Georgia with standard system fallbacks, Arial interface type, Courier section labels.
- `public/search.css` and `public/news.css`: corresponding search and news treatments.
- `public/app.js`: masthead, numbered navigation, county colophon, court notebook, court listings, and revised short copy.

The mark is vector source, not stock artwork or an image-generator output. It was drawn through AI-assisted code. The fonts are existing system typefaces, not proprietary Courtside type designs. No trademark search or registration has been performed. No copyright registration has been filed.

## Authorship and future protection

These files preserve the implementation and its history; they are not evidence that Eric personally drew the logo or wrote the new code and copy. AI-created expressive material must not be represented as human-authored merely because Eric requested, selected, or approved it. Human-authored contributions, sufficiently creative human modifications, and qualifying selection or arrangement should be identified accurately. A style, palette, name, slogan, basic gavel shape, typeface, factual court directory, and public-domain rules do not become exclusive copyright property through this redesign.

For a human designer's further development, retain sketches, dated source versions, the designer's actual contributions, and a written rights assignment where appropriate. For registration, claim only qualifying human authorship and disclose/exclude AI-generated material as applicable. Brand identity may also warrant trademark review, which is separate from copyright.
