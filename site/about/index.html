Commercial litigation. Trials. Arbitration. Appeals.

Eric Hudson is a litigation partner at Arambula Terrazas PLLC whose practice centers on complex commercial, construction, and employment disputes. He brings nearly two decades of experience to business conflicts, trials, arbitration, injunctions, and appeals.

Eric serves as first-chair counsel in state and federal courts, arbitration, and administrative proceedings. His commercial practice spans contract and payment disputes, construction claims, trade secrets, employment matters, and business governance. He has prosecuted and defended business claims through jury trials and arbitration, pursued emergency relief to protect client interests and preserve assets, and handled related appeals. His experience includes representing businesses across the construction, manufacturing, real estate, and professional-services sectors.

Before joining Arambula Terrazas, Eric served as Senior Special Counsel in the Texas Attorney General’s Special Litigation Unit, handling constitutional and election litigation. His appellate experience includes the Texas Supreme Court and Fifth Circuit, as well as amicus representation before the U.S. Supreme Court.

Constitutional and religious liberty work

Alongside his commercial practice, Eric represents individuals and institutions in disputes over religious exercise, equal treatment, and governmental authority. That work includes education, land use, regulatory enforcement, and the freedom to carry out religious practices. It is part of a broader practice serving clients whose rights, businesses, and institutions are at stake.

Selected religious liberty matters and media coverage

Eric served as counsel of record for the Legal Insurrection Foundation in Mahmoud v. Taylor, a U.S. Supreme Court case about religious exercise in public schools. He filed an amicus brief in support of the petitioners.

In coverage of an education case, Eric told The Texas Tribune and ProPublica, “Religious liberty is not a temporary pass issued after a lawsuit.” His work on disputes involving state investigations and land use has also been covered by KERA News and the Houston Chronicle. Texas Monthly’s October investigation examined the state’s securities case.

Eric also represents the East Plano Islamic Center (EPIC) in its federal lawsuit against the Texas Funeral Service Commission, a case concerning the right to carry out religious funeral rites. KERA News covered the case in an article republished by Texas Public Radio.
